The problem

Matching drivers to demand isn't guesswork — it's a data problem.

Uber's dispatch bases need to know, ahead of time, when and where ride demand will spike so they can position active vehicles accordingly. Without that visibility, bases either over-supply during quiet hours or under-supply during rush — both cost money and hurt rider experience.

This project analyzes trip counts and active vehicle activity across dispatch bases for January and February to answer three questions: when do trips peak, which bases carry the most load, and how efficiently is each base using its fleet.

The dataset

Uber-Jan-Feb-Cleaned.xlsx

Raw trip data was parsed, cleaned, and aggregated in Python — splitting timestamps into date, weekday, and month fields, and validating trip/vehicle counts — before being fed into Tableau for visualization.

Insights

What the data revealed

01

Weekdays consistently outperform weekends in total trip volume, suggesting commute-driven demand rather than purely leisure travel.

02

A small number of dispatch bases dominate total ride activity, meaning fleet resources are better spent concentrated rather than spread evenly.

03

Trips per vehicle dip slightly on weekends — vehicles are on the road but less efficiently utilized than on weekdays.

04

Overall trip volume grew from January into February, a trend worth tracking against seasonal or promotional factors.
